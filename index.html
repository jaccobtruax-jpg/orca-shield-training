Messaging training recap

Everything we drilled in the last two weeks, compressed to the rules that change reply rates.

The standard, in one breath

Comment on their work first. Two-line DM. One open question they can answer cheaply. CTA at the tier that fits. Humanizer pass before send: no em dashes, no AI tells, varied rhythm. The product enters only after a signal. Never pitch-slap an inbox.

Yi's messaging rule (2026-10-21)

  • Keep it short. One idea, three lines max.
  • Focus on the model and its offensive capabilities. What it does, not what it is.
  • Include the evals. Numbers front-loaded.
  • Say it plugs into any harness or agent. That is the integration story buyers check.
  • Own the model. "We built", "we tasked it", "we scored". Never "according to the manufacturer".
  • No AI slop. No "delve", no "landscape", no "I hope this finds you well".
  • Match the X voice. Evidence first, numbers front-loaded, punchy.

Fluff-to-Fact

Stop selling products. Start solving math problems. Every message should carry at least one concrete number the buyer can verify: price per million tokens, a benchmark score, a latency figure, a context window. Fluff is a claim without a number. Fact is a claim with one attached.

Orca Shield deep dive

The main event. Read this twice before the session: once for the story, once for the honesty rules at the bottom.

The 30-second pitch

"AI agents are attacking websites now, not just scanning them. Most defenses try to spot the agent and block it. Shield flips that: it lures attack agents into decoys, feeds them synthetic files and credentials, and blocks the ones that bite. Every step an agent spends in a decoy is a step it does not spend on your real app. You start in Watch mode: one async tag, about two minutes, nothing blocked, and you can see which AI agents are already scouting your site. Included on eligible Team and Enterprise plans."

What Shield is

AI-native defense against attack agents. A deception layer for websites. It does not try to fingerprint an attacker's tooling. It plants lures only a probing agent goes looking for, serves synthetic files and dependencies so the agent maps an app that is not yours, traps it in an isolated decoy session walled off from real users, and blocks hard when it uses a decoy credential. Connect your WAF and a followed lead blocks the agent's IP at your edge too.

The threat it answers

Browser agentsAutomated browsers and AI agents that scout, click, and probe real apps at scale.
Source-inspection agentsAgents that read HTML, JS bundles, and dependencies to map your stack.
Credential testersAgents that try leaked or guessed credentials against every endpoint they find.
WAF blind spotsSignature-based WAFs miss fresh agent behavior; Shield feeds your WAF the IPs that took the bait.

How the deception layer works

1LureDecoy leads only a probing agent goes looking for.
2MisdirectSynthetic files and dependencies, so it maps an app that is not yours.
3TrapAn isolated decoy session, walled off from your real users.
4BlockUse a decoy credential and Shield blocks it. WAF-connected, a followed lead blocks its IP.

The rollout: Watch first, protect later

1 · Watch
Start here. One line, about two minutes. See which AI agents scout your app. Nothing is blocked or diverted. No page load depends on us, no DNS, release, or script swap.
2 · Decoys at your edge
Your CDN or edge. Decoys live on paths real users never request. Your bundle stays on your hosting.
3 · Full protection
Advanced. Script swap: serve your bundle through Shield. Your pages then depend on it, and the decoy layer is complete.

What Activity records, and what it cannot

  • Highlighted results are what Activity records: visits that engaged with decoys.
  • Some automated browsers produce a reviewable signal. Stealth or non-browser agents may produce none.
  • A hint alone never blocks or diverts a request. Blocking happens on decoy credential use, or on a followed lead once your WAF is connected.
  • Source-inspection agents may investigate synthetic files and dependencies. A capable agent can ignore them; no guaranteed slowdown is claimed.

Sales angles

Claim hygiene: what we can and cannot say

The cyber page is explicit and reps must stay inside it. We can say Shield lures, misdirects, traps, and blocks. We cannot say it catches every agent: stealth or non-browser agents may produce no signal. A hint alone never blocks or diverts a request; blocking is tied to decoy credential use or a WAF-connected lead. Capable agents can ignore decoys, and no guaranteed slowdown is claimed. The three-visits illustration is an illustration, not a measurement. Say all of it in our own words: "we built", "we report", never "according to the manufacturer".

Objection scripts

"Deception sounds like a honeypot, and honeypots have a history of failing."
Fair, and different. Honeypots are usually manual, static, and isolated from your production edge. Shield is a productized deception layer with a watch-first rollout: stage one is one tag, nothing blocked, and you see the scout traffic on your own app before you commit to anything. Blocking only fires on decoy credential use or a WAF-connected lead. It is defensive tooling you can run incrementally, not a research project.
"We already have a WAF."
Keep it. A WAF matches known attack patterns; it does not know what a fresh AI agent probing with new credentials looks like. Shield creates the events your WAF can act on: when an agent follows a decoy lead, Shield can block its IP at your edge through the WAF connection. Different layers: the Firewall governs what your own agents do, Shield governs what attacking agents do to your site.
"A capable agent can just ignore the decoys."
True, and the honest version is part of our pitch: no guaranteed slowdown is claimed. The value is not that every agent is fooled. It is that probing agents that take the bait burn their steps in decoys instead of your real app, and using a decoy credential is a hard block signal plus an IP your WAF can drop. Watch first tells you whether your site is even being scouted.
"We are not seeing attack agents today."
Then Watch answers the question in two minutes with one tag and nothing blocked. If scout traffic shows up, you have your reason to add decoys. If it does not, you have clean evidence for your security review that the site is not being probed. Either outcome is a win, and it costs almost nothing to run the experiment.
"Another security tool to manage."
It is a section of the console you already pay for, at /console/shield, not a new vendor. Stage one is one async tag with no page-load dependency and no DNS or script swap. It wires into the WAF you already run instead of competing with it. The management cost is closer to flipping on a feature than adopting a product.
"What about our real traffic and real users?"
Decoys sit on paths real users never request, with synthetic files and fabricated credentials. Real traffic is untouched and never diverted. Watch records highlighted results only; a hint alone never blocks or diverts a request. Your bundle stays on your hosting until you choose the script-swap tier, and even then the choice is yours.

10-minute demo flow

  1. (2 min) Open /console/shield on a test site. Show the Watch entry: one async tag, about two minutes, nothing blocked. Paste it in.
  2. (2 min) Drive an automated browser at the site. Show Activity recording the probe: a reviewable signal, clearly highlighted. Note the honesty rule: stealth agents may produce none.
  3. (2 min) Flip on stage two, decoys at the edge. Walk the decoy paths real users never request. Show the synthetic files and dependencies the agent would map.
  4. (2 min) Show the block: an agent tries a decoy credential, Shield blocks it. Connect the WAF and show the IP drop at the edge.
  5. (2 min) Recap the four verbs: Lure, Misdirect, Trap, Block. Close with the Watch-first ask: one tag, two minutes, and we will tell you what is already scouting your site.

Demo cautions: the console needs a workspace login, so sign in before the meeting starts. Use a test site, never a customer's production app. Do not demo stage three (script swap) as a default; it makes pages depend on Shield and is an explicit buyer choice.
